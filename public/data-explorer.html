About the Data

Understanding the dataset!

Trying to predict credit card default risk by learning from historical financial and repayment behaviour. Click below to get an overview of the dataset, what “default” means, and the kinds of features used by the model.

Dataset Snapshot

30,000 Clients
23 Features
Binary Task Type
0 Missing Values

Project Purpose

  • Identify the key drivers behind credit card default and estimate the likelihood that a client will default based on their financial and behavioural profile.
  • Learn patterns from historical credit data including repayment history, credit usage and billing behaviour.
  • Use those patterns to make predictions for new or unseen client profiles.
  • Provide an estimated credit risk level for a client based on their financial behaviour.

Dataset Background

  • UCI "Default of Credit Card Clients" dataset
  • Originally used in study by Yeh & Lien (2009)
  • 30,000 credit card clients in Taiwan
  • Binary target: default vs non-default next month

What is Credit Card Default?

Missed Payments

Credit card default occurs when a cardholder repeatedly fails to make required payments over an extended period of time ☹

Financial Consequences

This can lead to penalties, interest accumulation, account restrictions or closure, other financial stress.

Credit Impact

Defaults can significantly damage a person's credit rating, making it harder to obtain loans or financial services.

Why It Matters

Understanding default risk helps financial institutions evaluate creditworthiness and manage lending risk.

Feature Categories

Click a category to learn what kind of information the model uses.

Demographics

Demographic variables help describe the client profile. While they do not explain credit risk on their own, they can interact with repayment and financial behaviour to reveal meaningful patterns.

Credit Information

Credit limit provides context on the client’s available credit. When combined with bill and payment amounts, it can reveal how heavily a client may be using their available credit.

Repayment History

Repayment status across multiple months can be a strong signal in credit risk modeling, since repeated delays can indicate greater financial stress or unstable repayment behaviour.

Billing Data

Monthly statement amounts can capture spending and balance patterns. Sudden increases or consistently high balances may be useful when estimating risk.

Payment Behaviour

Previous payment amounts show how much the client has actually been paying. Comparing bills to payments can help measure repayment consistency.

Why this matters

1

Historical records

The model learns from patterns in past client behaviour.

2

Feature relationships

Repayment status, bills, and payments are transformed into useful predictors.

3

Risk estimation

For a new client profile, CredCheck applies learned patterns to estimate the likelihood of default.

So we're not just asking “who defaults?” but trying to understand the financial patterns that are most associated with higher default risk ☹
